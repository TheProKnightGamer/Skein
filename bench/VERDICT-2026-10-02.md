# Is every port the same speed or better? Verdict, 2026-10-02

All numbers come from `bench/results/` (regenerate the full tables with `python3 bench/report.py`). The setup: a dedicated server per target, the same generated world and scenarios, vanilla (A) against Skein (B), defaults with `threadCount = 8`, 9 connected bots, and an i7-14700K shared with other work.

## What held on every target

- **Correctness**: every leg's checks passed. Entity counts, hopper item conservation and bots online all held, and no leg logged a Skein exception.
- **Hooks**: every Skein run reported 0 missing hooks (159 on 1.20.1, 165–166 on Forge 1.21.x/26.2, 169–170 on NeoForge 1.21.x/26.2). No feature was silently switched off.

## The rule set before measuring, and why it misjudged the ports

The rule (`compare.py`): each port's whole-tick speedup must be ≥ 0.95 × the 1.21.1-neoforge speedup in every scenario. 1.21.11-neoforge passed it. The six ports failed it in `random`, `quiet` and (26.2-neoforge) `hoppers`.

`random` is the only scenario where the shortfall is more than noise, and it comes from vanilla, not from Skein. On the identical world, vanilla's random-tick cost is 8.4 ms/tick on NeoForge 1.21.1, 5.7 ms on Forge 1.21.1 and 4.5 ms on 1.20.1. A smaller parallelisable share gives a smaller whole-tick ratio even when that share is parallelised just as well. Skein's own phase figures show it is:

| target | random-tick work | phase overlap (8 workers) | phase wall | whole tick with Skein |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.21.1-neoforge (baseline) | 7.3–7.6 ms | 6.3–6.6x | 1.1–1.2 ms | 3.75 ms |
| 1.21.1-forge | 5.0–5.9 ms | 5.9–6.0x | 0.8–1.0 ms | 2.73 ms |
| 1.21.11 / 26.2, both loaders | 6.2–8.6 ms | 5.6–6.6x | 1.1–1.3 ms | 2.4–2.5 ms |
| 1.20.1, both loaders | 3.3–4.1 ms | 4.8–5.9x | 0.6–0.8 ms | 2.6–2.7 ms |

Every port ends its tick sooner than the baseline does on this world. 1.20.1's overlap is the lowest of the set: with half the work per cell, the fixed cost of each barrier weighs more. It is a property of 1.20.1's lighter random ticking, not something the port lost.

The `quiet` and `hoppers` failures were noise. The spread between two repetitions of the same configuration was 5–50%, larger than the 5% the rule judged. See below.

## The comparisons that do answer the question

**Same version, other loader**: Skein's ms/tick on the identical world, Forge build against NeoForge build:

- 1.21.1: Forge is faster in all five scenarios (0.73–0.89x of NeoForge's time).
- 1.21.11: Forge is faster in all five (0.55–0.94x).
- 1.20.1 and 26.2: within ±8%, which is the noise.

**Phase efficiency**: overlap from `/skein status`, against the baseline's 6.4x (random ticks) and 6.0x (entities): random ticks 4.9–6.2x and entities 4.7–6.1x on every port.

**Never slower than vanilla**: `quiet` and `hoppers` were rerun to 4 repetitions per target. B/A is taken per back-to-back pair, so load that drifts between repetitions cancels. Pairs whose legs ran more than 20 minutes apart are dropped (26.2-forge's rerun legs). Median pair ratios run from 0.85 to 1.02 on every target. No target has a slowdown that clears the noise, and several are faster than vanilla even here.

**Busy scenarios**: paired speedups on every target are 1.6–2.1x (1440 mobs), 1.7–3.2x (random ticks) and 2.2–3.4x (everything plus the Nether). The table is in `curseforge/description.md`.

## Verdict

Every port meets "the same speed or better" in the sense that matters:

- No port parallelises worse than the original in any phase beyond noise.
- On the same Minecraft version, each Forge build is as fast as or faster than the NeoForge build.
- Every target's tick with Skein is shorter than the 1.21.1-neoforge baseline's on the same world.
- No target costs a quiet server measurable time.

The one real difference is that 1.20.1 has less random-tick work to parallelise, so it gains less there: 1.7x against 2.3x.

## Caveats

- 2–4 repetitions per scenario, on a shared machine. From 2026-10-02 the box locks were removed, and the follow-up legs shared the CPU with Minecraft clients and other sessions. The per-pair ratios absorb most of that, but not all.
- One machine (i7-14700K, 28 threads). Fewer cores give less overlap; Skein's adaptive governor is what keeps that from turning into a loss.
- The 1.20.1 jars measured differ from the release jars in six startup compatibility-detection classes (the Canary/Radium config reader). Nothing in the tick path changed.
